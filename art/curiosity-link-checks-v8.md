## Source and wording notes

- Gateway is the Army’s 1983 assessment preserved by the Government Publishing Office. Its presence in a government archive is not proof of its claims; the link description makes that distinction explicit.
- The Stargate link is the CIA’s own historical account of remote-viewing research and its decision not to restore the program. It does not claim psychic spying was established science.
- The Versailles time-slip account is a historical firsthand story. The description explicitly calls the claim unverified. The NIST and NASA entries address time dilation separately.
- The Hottel memo is a second- or third-hand report that the FBI describes as unconfirmed. The link description retains that distinction.
- The Kryptos description does not claim its fourth section is still unsolved. Recent developments make that wording unreliable even though the CIA museum description retains older wording.
- Tesla’s FBI files are presented as released records, not proof of hidden working weapon technology.
- The Codex Gigas page is its manuscript catalogue. The name “Devil’s Bible” does not assert a supernatural origin.
- DARPA’s NESD page is marked complete and no longer maintained. Descriptions use historical program language and make no claim that research goals were achieved.
